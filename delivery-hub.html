Overview

Onboarding for a delivery app: fast, safe, reliable.

Delivery Hub connects people with couriers and fast delivery services. I designed the first-run experience end to end — the moment a new user decides whether to trust the app with a package — from flow to high-fidelity screens and a clickable prototype.

Users
First-time senders, returning customers
Core features
Sign up, delivery details, live tracking, confirmation
My scope
Research, user flow, wireframes, UI, prototype
02

Problem

New users need a clear, reassuring way to begin using a delivery service.

Trust comes first

Handing over a package means trusting a stranger with it. The first screens have to feel safe.

Too many first steps

Long sign-up forms and unclear next steps push new users away before their first booking.

Waiting is stressful

Once a package is sent, people want to know where it is without having to call anyone.

How might we…help a first-time user go from opening the app to a tracked delivery, feeling confident at every step?

04

Insights

What we learned — and what I designed in response.

  1. 01

    Trust is decided in seconds

    People judge a delivery service on its first screen.

    Design responseA warm courier-handover illustration and a clear “Fast · Safe · Reliable” promise.
  2. 02

    Forms feel like work

    Every extra field at sign-up is a reason to leave.

    Design responseThree-field sign-up, with “Sign in” one tap away for returning users.
  3. 03

    Addresses go wrong

    Unclear address entry leads to failed or late deliveries.

    Design responseAddress fields grouped the way people write them: name, address, city, postal code.
  4. 04

    Silence creates anxiety

    Not knowing where a package is drives calls and worry.

    Design responseA live tracking timeline with times for every status.
05

Persona

Designing for Ada.

Ada, 29
Small-business owner · sends packages weekly

“I just want to send it, know where it is, and know it arrived.”

Goals
  • Book a delivery in a few minutes
  • Know exactly where her package is
  • Get proof that it was delivered
Frustrations
  • Long sign-up forms before she can do anything
  • Calling couriers to ask for updates
  • Wrong or incomplete addresses causing delays
Needs
  • A quick, reassuring first run
  • Clear real-time tracking
  • A confirmation she can share with customers

12

Lessons learned

What I’d carry into the next project.

  • Trust is a design problemTone, illustration and clear terms do as much for confidence as any feature.
  • Ask only for what you needCutting sign-up to three fields kept the focus on the first delivery, not the form.
  • Design the waiting, not just the bookingTracking is where people spend the most time with a delivery app, so it deserved the same care.
